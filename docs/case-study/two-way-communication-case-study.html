01 · Summary

Developers could open a case, but not follow it.

Email carried the whole conversation. Replies came back by mail, every follow-up opened a new case, and the only history was the developer’s own inbox.

I joined a plan that was a year old and, in three weeks of design, introduced case management into the two-way conversation, built on five decisions. In Phase 2 I proposed an AI help widget, which the contact-support platform team built.

02 · Problem

A case was never a thing a developer could hold.

The brief was two-way communication. The deeper gap: once created, a case lived almost entirely in email. There was no case page, no status, no history, nothing to return to. Developers could not tell whether anyone was working on their issue.

Follow-ups became new cases
A reply by email could only be answered through a new form, so one problem produced several tickets and inflated volume.
History lived in inboxes
Reviewing a case meant searching mail by hand, harder with several apps and several open issues.
Support was fragmented
Five teams used seven tools to write to developers, in different formats, priorities and sender domains.
Internal tracking was weak
Partner managers saved emails by hand to beat the retention policy. An urgent request was invisible if their manager was away.
No one could see patterns

Silence did as much damage as slowness
Forums and sentiment studies named “responsiveness” and “support” as the main negative drivers.

Two kinds of developer

Managed partners have a named partner manager and usually write to that person. Unmanaged developers have one route: the Contact Us form, the front door for most of the base.

Several teams behind the form

Cases split almost evenly between developer relations and content quality; about four in five quality cases were about content policy. Content quality asked for licences and certificates by email, but developers could not reply.

04 · Approach

A phased plan, a launch-and-learn release.

Insight. The real gap was the “submit and wait” model, not a missing reply box. Silence did as much damage as slowness, so most of the drop in cases would come from removing the reasons to open a second one.

The plan I inherited
Written in late 2023. Phase 1: a case tool on Contact Us, with create, status, history and replies from console or email. Phase 2: the help widget I proposed. Phase 3: trend analysis and automation.
Launch and learn
Release to a random share of unmanaged developers first. A two-way door that allows comparison with the old flow.
Protect managed partners
Their flow was left alone. They emailed a dedicated address that created a case in the same tool: a new address, not a new habit.
Keep the forum
Crowd-sourced answers are convenient, so the community forum was out of scope.
Rejected
A read-only viewer on top of email would not remove the follow-up-as-new-ticket problem. A chat-first model sketched in a late-2023 workshop was a much larger change, not on the roadmap.

05 · Solution

Five decisions.

Three weeks is short, so the job was choosing what the experience had to get right.

Decision 01

The console holds the conversation, and email mirrors it

A case page is now the record: every message, attachment and detail, newest first, each labelled by who sent it, with the reply box at the bottom. After submitting, developers land on this page and get a confirmation email with the case ID.

  • Replies from console and email land in the same conversation, so nobody has to change habit.
  • Details panel: case ID, status, category, related apps, device, creator, dates.
  • Resolve from the page and re-open for 30 days, so closing is safe, not final.
Decision 02

Status is the headline, written from the developer’s side

The original spec listed open, pending marketplace action, pending developer action and closed. What shipped puts the question on the label: pending your action, pending support action, resolved.

  • A developer scanning the list sees whose move it is without opening the case.
  • Search by case ID or app, sort by column, filter by app, status or creator.
  • A line above the table states details stay available for 12 months.
Decision 03

Collect context up front, and accept a longer form

Support’s first reply was often a question: which app, which package, when. I moved those questions into the form so the first response can be an answer.

  • Category, then topic replaces one vague dropdown.
  • Related apps: searchable, multi-select, chosen apps as removable chips.
  • Description: opens with prompts for what support needs first. Limits: 4,000 characters, subject 250, attachment 5 MB.
  • Access: share by role or name. “View my cases” comes before a new one.

The cost. A longer form could raise abandonment. The plan tracked drop-off for that reason. I have no result to quote.

Decision 04

Put cases where developers already are

“My Cases” now leads the support links in the Help menu, and every app has a Communications entry in its side navigation with a count of what is new.

  • Support Requests lists the app’s cases; Communications holds what the marketplace sent.
  • Each tab has its own unread count; a dot marks a new update.
  • An empty tab points to the full list, never a dead end.
Decision 05

Adopt the ecosystem now, plan the exit, and let help end in a case

There was pushback on leaving the company ecosystem. I did not argue it: I designed within it and wrote a staged plan for moving to our own contact-support experience.

  • For Phase 2 I proposed a chat widget that answers first.
  • With no answer, it opens a case, gives the case ID and links to it, so nothing asked is lost.
  • Built by the contact-support platform team; its design is theirs.
06 · Held back

Two things left out on purpose.

Promising a resolution date
A date we miss costs more trust than silence, but silence leaves a developer in a black box. The team chose to wait until response targets were reliable, then show an estimated time.
Trend analysis and deeper automation
A single record makes it possible to ask which issues recur. Later phases aimed to use that for pre-emptive help. It was out of reach until the record existed.

08 · Reflection

The most useful change was a model change.

From “submit and wait” to “open a thread”. Most of the drop came from removing the reasons to open a second case.

Measure helped, not just fewer
Satisfaction after a deflected question, form drop-off, how often resolved cases are re-opened.
Bring back estimated resolution time
Once response targets hold; a status label only half solves the black box.
Grow the Communications tab
Into one role-aware home for everything the marketplace tells developers. A direction, not something I shipped.

Working inside a design system I did not own taught me where design leverage sits when styling is fixed: in what is shown, in what order, and whose move each state says it is.
